# MindCraft — Design Principles

> **The non-negotiable philosophy.** Every visual, copy, interaction, and structural decision is measured against this file first. Implementation-grade tokens and components live in `CLAUDE.md` and `/specs/19`. This file is the **why** and the **guardrails** that those files must never contradict.

---

## What MindCraft actually is

Design must emerge from this, not from any other company's template:

```
AFRICAN IN CONTEXT        — Nairobi, Africa, 45 countries, authentic business context
GLOBAL IN AMBITION        — Europe + United States reach, international standards
DEEP IN LIFE SCIENCES     — founding heritage, 6 sub-sectors, sector-specific AI
BROAD IN BUSINESS CAPABILITY — four integrated pillars as one engine
RIGOROUS IN RESEARCH      — market intelligence, primary + secondary, MEL
PRACTICAL IN CONSULTING   — strategy that leads to implementation
MODERN IN TECHNOLOGY      — AI enterprise solutions that actually get deployed
RESPONSIBLE IN AI         — ethics, governance, privacy by design
EDITORIAL IN COMMUNICATION — clarity, structure, measured language
HUMAN IN JUDGMENT         — a consultancy, not a product
```

The design translates all of the above into visible form. Every element must be traceable to one of these qualities.

## Africa as principle, not decoration

Africa is a **strategic position** in MindCraft's identity — not a visual theme.

**Africa communicates through:**
- Geographic intelligence (4 regions, 45 countries, 12 focus markets)
- Market systems and regulatory knowledge
- Business institutions and cities
- Healthcare infrastructure
- Nairobi's position as a business and innovation hub
- Cultural and commercial fluency
- People at work in real African professional contexts

**Africa does NOT communicate through:**
- Safari imagery or wildlife
- Continent silhouettes used decoratively
- "Tribal" patterns or cultural stereotypes
- Generic colorful flags
- Poverty narratives
- Exoticized or romanticized photography
- The word "Africa" used as a marketing adjective

The visual test: remove all text. Is the African context still readable through composition, information architecture, and geographic intelligence? If yes — success. If no — the identity depends on a label, not on genuine design.

## Life Sciences as architectural depth

Life Sciences is MindCraft's **founding heritage** — not one of many equal sectors.

The design must make this asymmetry visible:

```
Life Sciences:   DEEP — dedicated page, ecosystem of 6 sub-sectors,
                        focus areas per sub-sector, sector-specific AI,
                        "Core expertise" signal
Other sectors:   BROAD — filterable grid, capability mapping,
                         no fabricated sector depth
```

The design communicates: **"Deep in Life Sciences. Broad across industry."**

If every industry looks equally deep, the design has failed. Life Sciences must look like it has more layers, more language, more intelligence behind it. Because it does.

## The four capabilities as one engine

The four pillars must feel **integrated**, not parallel:

```
MARKET RESEARCH  (understand the market)
      ↓
BUSINESS CONSULTING  (decide the strategy)
      ↓
AI ENTERPRISE SOLUTIONS  (implement with AI)
      ↓
AI TRAINING  (embed capability)
      ↓
MEASURABLE IMPACT → loops back to the next question
```

A visitor should understand: *"I don't need four vendors. I need one partner who can take me from question to impact."*

**Design implications:**
- The four pillars are entered from a **question or outcome**, not from a menu
- They are displayed as a **connected spine**, not four equal cards
- Their relationship is more important than their individual descriptions
- Cross-links between pillars are structural, not optional

## Content type → visual form

Do not default to cards. Match the visual form to what the information actually is:

| Information type | Right form | Wrong form |
|---|---|---|
| Capability system | Connected spine / editorial flow | Four cards |
| Process | Process spine (horizontal or vertical) | Numbered cards |
| Geography | Map + regional selector | Country text block |
| Life Sciences | Ecosystem diagram | Six cards |
| Industry taxonomy | Structured list with expansion | Grid of equal cards |
| Differentiators | Editorial statement blocks | Icon + title + body cards |
| Data / metadata | Restrained data strip | Decorative charts |
| Audience pathways | Self-selection module | A list of logos |
| CTA / conversion | Strong typographic ink band | A button surrounded by filler |
| Explorer (I×G×C) | Interactive composer | SaaS-style filter dashboard |

## The "do not card everything" rule

Cards communicate: *"these are separate, parallel, equal items."*

Most information on this site is NOT that. It is:
- Connected (capabilities)
- Hierarchical (industries — some deeper than others)
- Sequential (process)
- Geographic (regions, not cards)
- Editorial (statements, not boxes)

**Use cards only when the information is genuinely a set of equally-weighted, enumerable objects.** (Examples: the ~19 industry breadth chips; the six Life Sciences sub-sectors when selectable.) Even then: flat, hairline-bordered, minimal radius.

## Progressive disclosure: the information architecture principle

The site operates on a strict disclosure ladder:

```
SCAN       [always visible] — one line, one number, one visual
UNDERSTAND [always visible] — 1–3 sentence editorial statement
EXPLORE    [user action]    — selecting a node, card, region, filter
DETAIL     [revealed]       — accordion, panel, drawer, or dedicated page
ENGAGE     [contextual CTA] — specific to the item explored
```

**No two adjacent layers may carry equal density.** SCAN content must be scannable. DETAIL content can go deep. The failure mode is SCAN-level content that contains DETAIL-level copy.

## The visual language: Editorial Intelligence

**Inspired by:** premium strategy/research publishing, intelligence platforms, institutional research, not SaaS landing pages.

**Material system:**
- Warm paper / ivory base (never cool gray, never pure white)
- Deep ink text (not pure black — warm, slightly brown-leaning)
- Single terracotta/sienna accent — scarce, meaningful
- Hairline rules as structure (not drop shadows)
- Ink bands for emphasis (2 maximum per page)
- Editorial asymmetry where it adds meaning

**Typography:**
- Display: **Bricolage Grotesque** — weight 300–500, negative letter-spacing, fluid scale
- Body: **Literata** — warm reading serif, carries depth and intelligence
- Mono: **Spline Sans Mono** — eyebrows, indices, coordinates, data labels (sparing)

**What the typography communicates:** "We document complex things precisely." A research publication voice, not a product voice.

**Banned font list** (AI monoculture defaults — never use): Inter · Fraunces · Cormorant · DM Sans · Playfair Display · Space Mono · IBM Plex (any variant) · Instrument (any variant) · Lora · Syne · Plus Jakarta Sans · Outfit · Roboto.

## Anti-AI-slop: the banned patterns

**Visual (absolute bans):**
- Gradient text (`background-clip: text` + gradient background) — never
- Purple / blue / cyan AI gradients — never
- Glowing blobs, glowing orbs, glow effects as decoration
- Glassmorphism as brand texture
- Floating translucent cards with blur
- Excessive rounded cards (>8px radius as default)
- Giant 3D objects, abstract spheres, wireframe globes
- AI brain illustrations, neural-network diagrams as decoration
- Robot imagery of any kind
- Circuit-board graphics
- Animated particles, cursor trails
- Fake dashboards, fake terminal windows
- Decorative / meaningless data visualizations
- Colored left/right border stripes on cards (AI design tell — banned)
- Everything-in-a-card as the default layout
- Scroll-hijacking or gratuitous parallax

**Content (anti-fabrication bans):**
- Client logos or "Trusted by" sections
- Testimonials
- Case study statistics or outcome percentages
- "Proven" / "Leading" / track-record language
- Office locations beyond Nairobi HQ
- Deployment counts, project counts
- Partner logos
- Awards, certifications

**Language (banned):**
- "Unlock the power of AI"
- "AI-powered future"
- "Next-generation"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Get started" / "Learn more" (as generic CTAs — replace with specific actions)
- Any claim not supported by the two source documents

## The credibility model for a 2026 firm

MindCraft was founded in 2026. We never pretend otherwise.

Credibility comes from:
- **Specialization** — Life Sciences depth is real and evidenced by specific language
- **Methodology** — the process, the six stages, the integrated engine
- **Geographic intelligence** — 45 countries, 4 regions, Nairobi as anchor
- **Domain language** — KOL mapping, pharmacovigilance, reimbursement, MEL, RPA — specificity is credibility
- **Structural facts** — 4 pillars, 6 sub-sectors, 4 regions, 6 stages, 5 differentiators

These are real. Use them instead of fake social proof.

## The recognition test

Remove the MindCraft logo and all company text.

Can someone still identify the site as: *a serious African intelligence + consulting + AI capability firm*?

If yes — the design is doing its job.
If no — the visual identity depends on a label, not on design. Fix it.

Recognition must come through:
- Composition and grid choices
- Typography voice (editorial serif + distinctive grotesque)
- Information architecture (the engine, the ecosystem, the regional model)
- Material system (warm paper, hairlines, restrained terracotta)
- Editorial rhythm and whitespace
- Geographic intelligence (not decoration)

---

## Decision test (apply to every element)

1. Does it help the visitor understand MindCraft, or move toward engagement? → If no, cut.
2. Is it the *least* amount of visible content that still communicates the idea? → If no, simplify.
3. Would a Life Sciences director or African market strategist find this credible? → If no, redesign.
4. Is it honest and source-supported? → If no, remove or correct.
5. Does it work as well at 320px as at 1440px? → If no, it is not done.
6. Does it look like an AI generated it? → If yes, redesign.
7. Does Africa appear through intelligence or through decoration? → Decoration means redesign.

## Responsive principle

**REFLOW, NEVER REMOVE.**

Desktop compositions must transform intelligently for mobile. No amputation of content. No "desktop-first then squish." Every layout decision is made knowing it must adapt.

Mobile-specific behavior is designed first-class, not retrofitted. The mobile experience is not a degraded version of desktop; it is an equally intentional composition for a smaller canvas.

---

## Motion principle

Motion in this site **clarifies**. It does not perform.

Motion explains:
- Navigation transitions (where am I going?)
- Progressive disclosure (what is opening?)
- Explorer state changes (what did I select?)
- Geographic selection (what region am I viewing?)
- Process progression (where am I in the engagement model?)
- Content entry (the section is appearing, not already-present)

Motion does not explain: "this website is cool" or "this company uses AI."

`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is always respected.

---

## Accessibility principle

Accessibility is part of design, not an afterthought added after visual work is done.

Every color choice must pass contrast at its intended use. Every interactive element must be keyboard-operable. Every form must be usable by a screen reader. Every motion must be disableable. Every map and diagram must have a text alternative.

If it is not accessible, it is not done.

---

## Performance principle

The design must remain lightweight. Visual complexity that requires heavy technical infrastructure is a design failure, not a technical one. If the design concept requires WebGL, Three.js, Lottie, or large video to function — reconsider the concept.

Every visual element must pay for its performance cost with a proportionate design contribution.
